\section*{Read for a claim, an assumption and a test}
Read the teaching guide first, then use this pack alongside the lecture.
The annotations below are course-written paraphrases and investigation prompts,
not quotations from the papers. Each source link identifies a primary paper
or official guidance. Benchmark results do not transfer automatically to our
small exercises. For each reading, write the mathematical object, the needed
assumptions and one observation that would challenge your interpretation.

\section{Christiano et al. (2017): Human preferences}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03741}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} The paper learns a reward signal from pairwise trajectory preferences and uses it for policy learning. It makes preference collection and reward generalisation part of the feedback loop.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Why is a comparison probability a model of noisy preference rather than a deterministic ordering rule? Add the same constant to both scores and show the likelihood is unchanged. Use this invariance to discuss what a fitted score does and does not identify.

\section{Schulman et al. (2017): PPO}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} PPO proposes practical surrogate objectives for policy updates, including clipped probability ratios. The clipped objective limits the incentive to move selected sampled terms further; it does not constrain every parameter update or impose a hard KL budget.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Plot a single positive-advantage and negative-advantage term against the probability ratio. Which side flattens? Distinguish the old rollout policy from the fixed language reference policy. They support different ratios and KL terms.

\section{Ziegler et al. (2019): Language fine-tuning}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08593}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} This work adapts language models from human preferences with a reference-policy KL penalty and an adaptive coefficient. The coefficient responds to measured divergence; controller cadence and whether KL is per token or per sequence affect the update.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Write the sign of the coefficient update when observed KL exceeds its target. Check the units of sample count and controller horizon. The workbook uses one controller step at a time; it does not silently reproduce every scaling convention in the original training system.

\section{Ouyang et al. (2022): InstructGPT}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} InstructGPT combines supervised demonstrations, preference-based reward modelling and policy optimisation. The paper's evaluation compares particular models and feedback protocols, not all possible human values or all future use cases.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Draw three separate datasets and explain which loss each trains. Where can annotator preferences, prompt selection or reward-model error affect the final policy? The local sentiment experiment demonstrates the pipeline's mechanics, not the paper's model scale or evaluation.

\section{Gao et al. (2022): Reward overoptimisation}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} This empirical study compares optimisation of a proxy reward with evaluation by a stronger reward model. Its fitted scaling relationships describe studied regimes, rather than universal analytic laws of all reward misspecification.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Put proxy score and evaluation score on the same optimisation-budget axis. What observation would falsify the claim that more optimisation always helps? Explain why a held-out reward model is itself still a proxy rather than an infallible human-quality oracle.

\section{Bai et al. (2022): Constitutional AI}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} Constitutional AI uses principles in critique/revision and AI-generated preference feedback. Human choices remain in principles, model construction and evaluation; replacing some labels does not remove those normative inputs.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Trace how a principle becomes a training signal. Design a response that follows a superficial wording cue while violating the principle's intent. Compare held-out human assessment against agreement with the same model used to generate labels.

\section{Stiennon et al. (2020): Summarisation}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01325}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} The work studies summarisation trained with human feedback and reports evaluations beyond reward-model fit. It is a concrete setting for separating preference prediction accuracy from policy quality.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} What can happen if summaries grow more persuasive but less faithful? Name an evaluation that checks source-grounded factuality. A reward-model win rate alone does not establish that property.

\section{Lambert et al. (2024): Tulu 3}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} Tulu 3 documents a multi-stage open post-training recipe, including supervised tuning, preference optimisation and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards. Stages and datasets are explicit parts of its experimental design.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Separate the stage order from an arbitrary mixture of loss terms. Which changes would require a new ablation? The repository's optional LLM lab supplies SFT/DPO smoke workflows; the week's NumPy/PyTorch helpers are not a current universal TRL trainer API.

\section*{Connect the reading to your workbook}
Before running a policy update, hand-calculate a two-comparison Bradley--Terry loss, the per-token reference log ratio, and the final-token reward. Verify terminal value masking in GAE. Record the preference model, reference model and rollout-policy snapshots separately so their roles cannot collapse into one variable.
