\section*{Read for a claim, an assumption and a test}
Read the teaching guide first, then use this pack alongside the lecture.
The annotations below are course-written paraphrases and investigation prompts,
not quotations from the papers. Each source link identifies a primary paper
or official guidance. Benchmark results do not transfer automatically to our
small exercises. For each reading, write the mathematical object, the needed
assumptions and one observation that would challenge your interpretation.

\section{Burda et al. (2018): RND}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12894}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} RND rewards prediction error against a fixed randomly initialised target network. The predictor learns visited observations; the target must remain fixed. Novelty in representation space need not correspond to progress on the external task.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Check that target parameters receive no optimiser update. Revisit one observation repeatedly and track its prediction error. Then change the observation representation: why can new irrelevant pixels look novel? The exact normalisation convention is part of the algorithm, not a cosmetic unit change.

\section{Pathak et al. (2017): ICM}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.05363}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} ICM learns features through an inverse-dynamics objective and uses forward prediction error as intrinsic reward. The representation aims to emphasise controllable aspects, but that is a modelling choice rather than a universal immunity to irrelevant noise.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Compare predicting raw observations with predicting learned features. What information does the inverse action-prediction task preserve or discard? Design a noisy observation channel and identify which assumption would make it irrelevant to the learned representation.

\section{Badia et al. (2020): Agent57}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13350}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} Agent57 combines multiple exploration and long-horizon learning components with adaptive choice among policies. Its Atari result evaluates the complete system, not an isolated proof that a particular intrinsic bonus always improves exploration.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Build a component map: replay, recurrent state, intrinsic rewards, discount choices and policy selection. Which components are absent from the workbook? State one ablation needed before attributing the full system's result to RND-like novelty alone.

\section{Finn et al. (2017): MAML}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03400}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} MAML learns parameters that perform well after a small adaptation update on a task. The outer loss differentiates through the inner update in full MAML. First-order approximations omit part of that derivative.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} For $L_m(\theta)=\tfrac12(\theta-m)^2$, derive the adapted parameter and outer gradient. Explain the factor $(1-\alpha)^2$. In a softmax bandit, adding a constant to every logit changes no probability, so symmetry does not establish a unique optimal parameter vector.

\section{Laskin et al. (2022): Algorithm Distillation}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14215}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} Algorithm Distillation trains sequence models on learning histories so that adaptation can occur through context at inference. A history containing rewards and behaviour across episodes differs from a single successful trajectory.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Distinguish test-time context changes from gradient updates. Which data order exposes improvement rather than merely good behaviour? Evaluate on tasks held out from training and include a no-history ablation. A short sequence-classification toy is not proof that an optimiser has been fully recovered.

\section*{Connect the reading to your workbook}
Run novelty, external return and coverage diagnostics together. For meta-learning, keep adaptation and evaluation samples separate, then compare full and first-order meta-gradients on a scalar problem before scaling up. Novelty, task learning and across-task adaptation answer different questions.
