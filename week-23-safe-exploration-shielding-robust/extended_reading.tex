\section{Alshiekh et al. (2018): Shielding}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} Shielding restricts decisions using a formal specification and an environment abstraction. Correctness depends on the abstraction and on remaining inside a region from which safe continuation is possible.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Draw a state that is safe now but has no safe successor action. Why does checking only the next label fail to establish indefinite safety without additional reachability assumptions? The course grid's first-admissible-action filter differs from sampling a renormalised masked policy, even though both can preserve the same invariant.

\section{Dalal et al. (2018): Safety layers}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08757}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} A safety layer corrects proposed continuous actions using a learned local constraint model. The affine action projection is exact for the stated local inequality, not automatically for unknown nonlinear physical dynamics.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Derive the projection onto $b+w^\top a\leq0$ with a Lagrange multiplier. Handle $w=0$ explicitly: either every action is feasible or none is. Explain why adding epsilon to the denominator cannot create control authority or solve incompatible actuator bounds.

\section{Tobin et al. (2017): Visual domain randomisation}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06907}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} This paper randomises simulated visual appearance for transfer of object localisation. It is useful historical context for domain randomisation, but it is not a proof about the pendulum's sampled mass and length dynamics.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Separate visual variation, physical parameter variation and worst-case robust optimisation. Averages under a chosen randomisation distribution do not bound unseen extremes. Propose a held-out parameter grid and report both average and worst observed return.

\section{Sim-to-real survey (2025)}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13187}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} The survey maps sim-to-real approaches and potential roles for foundation models. A survey organises evidence from multiple settings; it does not establish that semantic representations universally remove the reality gap.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Choose one reviewed mechanism and identify its required observations, embodiment assumptions and evaluation domain. Which part could be represented in the course toy, and which would require a new experiment? Keep a claimed direction of research separate from a verified performance result.

\section{Reed and Lahijanian (2024): Learned shielding}
\noindent\textbf{Primary source.} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07359}

\paragraph{Mechanism and scope.} This work learns unknown dynamics with deep-kernel models and builds interval-MDP abstractions for shielding. Probabilistic assurance is tied to uncertainty estimation and formal synthesis assumptions.

\paragraph{Read, derive, investigate.} Trace how a prediction interval becomes a transition-probability set. What happens if the interval loses coverage under distribution shift? The exact deterministic grid model bypasses this learning problem; its zero-violation demonstration does not reproduce unknown-dynamics assurances.

\section*{Connect the reading to your workbook}
The notebook should distinguish execution safety from value estimation. The current tabular learner masks executed actions but maximises over all actions in its bootstrap. Compare that optimistic target with a maximum over safe next actions; preserve the terminal mask in both. An execution shield can still block a dangerous action while its unused optimistic value distorts learning.
