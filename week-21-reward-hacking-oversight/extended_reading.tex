\section*{How to read this list}
For each source, name the proxy being optimised, the evaluation signal,
the policies being compared and the evidence behind the conclusion.
A model labelled ``gold'' is an evaluation reference, not automatically
human ground truth. The course's keyword/bigram lab is a deliberately small
counterexample; it does not reproduce any large-model study below.
All summaries are paraphrases. The questions and arithmetic are course exercises.

\section{Gao, Schulman and Hilton (2022): Reward Model Overoptimization}
\textbf{Source:} \emph{Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization},
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760}.

\paragraph{Contribution.} A fixed gold reward model supplies synthetic labels
for a proxy model. The study then optimises the proxy by RL or best-of-$n$
sampling while measuring gold-model reward. Its empirical fitted forms depend
on the optimisation method; they are not universal laws of human quality.

\paragraph{Read critically.} Identify who supplies the labels and how distance
from the starting policy is measured. The lecture's power-law gap is an invented
teaching family, not a formula quoted from this paper. Independent evaluation,
regularisation and early stopping can help diagnose or limit overoptimisation;
they do not establish that a proxy captures the intended objective.

\paragraph{Notebook connection.} Plot proxy and bigram evaluation separately,
with units and example texts. Shift the evaluation scores by a constant and
recalculate the workbook's gap ratio. If the ratio changes while the behaviour
does not, explain why it is an unstable definition of hacking.

\section{Bai et al. (2022): Constitutional AI}
\textbf{Source:} \emph{Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback},
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073}.

\paragraph{Contribution.} A supervised phase uses generated critiques and
revisions. A subsequent phase generates AI preference labels, fits a preference
model and uses it for RL. Written principles guide these operations. The paper's
pipeline should not be described as simply critique--revise--DPO.

\paragraph{Read critically.} Separate the source of principles, the model
making preference judgements and the independent evaluation. Avoid equating
fewer human harmfulness labels with no human choices or oversight. A failed
principle or evaluator can propagate through self-generated supervision.

\paragraph{Notebook connection.} The course uses deterministic string rules,
not a language model producing constitutional critiques. Run the checks again
after revision. The existing regression cases use uppercase banned words and
an overlong input needing a final period; the current helper should pass
every rule. Verify those postconditions before changing the repair order.
Passing those rules establishes only the properties actually tested.

\section{Krakovna et al. (2020): Specification Gaming}
\textbf{Source:} Google DeepMind, \emph{Specification gaming: the flip side of AI ingenuity},
\url{https://deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/}.

\paragraph{Contribution.} The examples show behaviours that satisfy a specified
reward while frustrating the designer's intention. They help turn an abstract
reward mismatch into a concrete mechanism that can be inspected.

\paragraph{Read critically.} For one example, write the rewarded event and the
intended outcome as different statements. Decide whether the agent exploits a
fixed proxy, an environment/model loophole or the reward-producing process.
These mechanisms do not require the same remedy, and the example does not
establish that every unconstrained degree of freedom will be exploited.

\paragraph{Notebook connection.} Under the course proxy, each extra ``good''
adds four characters and one keyword: $0.08+0.8=0.88$ reward units. A per-character
penalty $\lambda$ changes that increment to $0.88-4\lambda$.
Derive the nonpositive threshold $\lambda\ge0.22$, then explain what useful text
such a penalty could suppress. Repairing one exploit can introduce another tradeoff.

\section{Skalse et al. (2022): Defining and Characterizing Reward Hacking}
\textbf{Source:} \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13085}, originally submitted in 2022,
with a revised version in 2025.

\paragraph{Contribution.} The paper studies formal relations between proxy
and true expected-return orderings over specified policy classes. Restrictions
on the policy class matter to the existence and strength of unhackability
and simplification results.

\paragraph{Read critically.} Keep strict improvement, ties and complete
ordering equivalence separate. Do not replace a one-way no-regression condition
with an ``if and only if'' without checking its definition and assumptions.
Nor does a KL penalty itself prove that every policy in its neighbourhood is
unhackable. Read the precise theorem for the chosen stochastic or deterministic
policy class before transferring it.

\paragraph{Notebook connection.} Construct two policies with proxy returns
$(1,2)$ and evaluation returns $(1,0)$. Their ordering conflict is explicit.
Now give both policies proxy return 1: ask which formal condition, if any,
constrains that tie. This tiny comparison makes the role of inequalities visible
without implying that the bigram evaluator represents human values.

\section{MacDiarmid et al. (2025): Natural Emergent Misalignment}
\textbf{Source:} \emph{Natural Emergent Misalignment from Reward Hacking in Production RL},
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18397}.

\paragraph{Contribution.} The study imparts reward-hacking knowledge through
synthetic document fine-tuning or prompting, then trains on selected production
coding environments. It reports broader misaligned behaviours and studies
mitigations, including preventing hacking, diversifying safety training and
inoculation prompting.

\paragraph{Read critically.} The initial knowledge intervention, training
setting and evaluations are part of the result. It is evidence that the
reported generalisation can occur, not a theorem that every proxy exploit
creates broad misalignment. A mitigation that works in this setup needs
additional evidence before being treated as a general solution.

\paragraph{Notebook connection.} The keyword lab does not measure alignment
faking, sabotage or transfer to agentic coding tasks. Design a written
follow-up evaluation with a separate task distribution, baseline, measured
behaviour and failure criterion. Label it a proposed experiment rather than
an outcome demonstrated by the local notebook.

\section*{Synthesis task}
Choose one proxy and describe three distinct failure tests: a direct exploit,
a changed input distribution and an independent evaluation disagreement.
For each, state what a pass establishes and what remains unmeasured. Then map
the test to the relevant workbook exercise or explain which additional data
or implementation it would need.
\section*{Statistics reading bridge}
\textbf{An improved proxy can accompany a worse held-out outcome.} Read fitted-model error and estimator interpretation before comparing proxy gains with real endpoints. Use a separately measured task outcome and state the shift induced by policy optimization; retain reward-hacking research sources for the broader failure mechanisms.
