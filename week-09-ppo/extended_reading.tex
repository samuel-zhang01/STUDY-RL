\section*{1.\ Schulman, Wolski, Dhariwal, Radford, Klimov (2017) --- Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms}
arXiv:1707.06347 (July 2017). 50,000+ citations as of 2026; the most-cited RL paper in history.

\paragraph{Summary.} Introduces the PPO-clip and PPO-KL variants. Argues that a first-order approximation to TRPO's trust region, combined with multiple epochs of mini-batch SGD, achieves comparable performance with $5\times$--$10\times$ less wall-clock. The paper has 6 pages of derivation and 4 of empirical results --- it is the shortest landmark paper in modern RL.

\paragraph{Why it matters for this week.} This is the algorithm we implement. Read Section 3 carefully; pay particular attention to the asymmetry of the clip with respect to the sign of $\hat\Adv$.

\paragraph{Annotated key passage.}
\begin{quote}\emph{``Note that, despite being a lower bound on $L^{\mathrm{CPI}}$ (and hence on the unclipped surrogate $\hat g$), $L^{\mathrm{CLIP}}$ is not a lower bound when $r_t < 1, \hat A_t > 0$, or $r_t > 1, \hat A_t < 0$ --- in these cases the clip is inactive.''}\end{quote}
This subtle remark is what makes the clip a soft trust region rather than a hard one. When the importance ratio moves toward $1$ from outside the box, the surrogate gradient is non-zero; only when it moves \emph{further away} does it saturate. The result is that PPO can ``re-enter'' the trust region even from a violation.

\section*{2.\ Huang, Dossa, Raffin, Kanervisto, Wang (2022) --- The 37 Implementation Details of Proximal Policy Optimization}
ICLR Blog Track 2022. \texttt{iclr-blog-track.github.io/2022/03/25/ppo-implementation-details/}

\paragraph{Summary.} A line-by-line forensic comparison of the original Schulman PPO code, the OpenAI Baselines reimplementation, the Stable Baselines3 reimplementation, and CleanRL. Identifies 37 distinct code-level details that affect performance --- the orthogonal init, the Adam $\varepsilon$, the value clipping, etc.

\paragraph{Why it matters for this week.} The checklist this week's mini-project follows is taken directly from this blog post. Without these details, the canonical PPO loss in Schulman 2017 does not converge.

\paragraph{Annotated key passage.}
\begin{quote}\emph{``In 2022, the difference between a paper PPO and a working PPO is 37 lines of code spread across 6 files. Each one is essential.''}\end{quote}
A useful corrective to ``it's just the loss''. The lesson generalises to every RL algorithm: there is no such thing as ``the algorithm'' in isolation; the code-level details are part of the algorithm.

\section*{3.\ Engstrom, Ilyas, Santurkar, Tsipras, Janoos, Rudolph, Madry (2020) --- Implementation Matters in Deep Policy Gradients}
ICLR 2020. arXiv:2005.12729.

\paragraph{Summary.} See week-8 extended reading. The headline result is reproduced here because it is the empirical justification for the 37-details exercise.

\paragraph{Why it matters for this week.} The strongest evidence in the literature that ``algorithm'' and ``implementation'' are fundamentally entangled in deep RL.

\paragraph{Annotated key passage.}
\begin{quote}\emph{``With the code-level optimisations from PPO added to TRPO, the two algorithms perform indistinguishably on all five MuJoCo benchmarks.''}\end{quote}
A controlled experiment: when you hold the optimisations fixed and vary only the algorithm, there is no measurable advantage to either. The corollary for practitioners: do not waste time tuning between PPO and TRPO; tune the 37 details first.

\section*{4.\ Andrychowicz, Raichuk, Stanczyk, Orsini, Girgin, Marinier, Hussenot, Geist, Pietquin, Michalski, Gelly, Bachem (2021) --- What Matters In On-Policy Reinforcement Learning? A Large-Scale Empirical Study}
ICLR 2021. arXiv:2006.05990.

\paragraph{Summary.} A sweep of 250,000 PPO/A2C/IMPALA runs on a battery of continuous-control tasks, ablating individual implementation choices. Findings: orthogonal init matters most for stability; advantage normalisation matters most for final performance; entropy bonus matters most for exploration; the choice of optimiser ($\varepsilon = 10^{-5}$ Adam vs.\ $10^{-8}$) genuinely affects convergence speed.

\paragraph{Why it matters for this week.} Quantifies the per-detail contribution. Useful when triaging a broken implementation: read this paper's Figure 4 and decide which detail to fix first.

\paragraph{Annotated key passage.}
\begin{quote}\emph{``The default $\varepsilon = 10^{-8}$ in Adam causes systematically worse performance than $\varepsilon = 10^{-5}$ across all five benchmarks we tested.''}\end{quote}
A single hyper-parameter buried in the optimiser, six orders of magnitude smaller than the learning rate, makes a measurable empirical difference. Such is deep RL.

\section*{5.\ Ouyang, Wu, Jiang, et al.\ (2022) --- Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback (InstructGPT)}
arXiv:2203.02155 (March 2022).

\paragraph{Summary.} The InstructGPT paper. PPO is used to fine-tune GPT-3 against a learned reward model trained on human preferences. The recipe (PPO + KL-to-reference penalty + per-token reward) is what underpins ChatGPT and every public LLM RLHF stack since.

\paragraph{Why it matters for this week.} This is the production application of PPO that dominates the modern landscape. Read sections 3.1--3.3; the PPO loss they write is exactly our \texttt{ppo\_loss}, with one extra reward-shaping term: $r_t \leftarrow r_t - \beta \log \Policy_\theta(a_t\given s_t) / \Policy_\text{ref}(a_t\given s_t)$.

\paragraph{Annotated key passage.}
\begin{quote}\emph{``We adapt the standard PPO algorithm to use a KL penalty between the policy and the SFT model as an additional reward shaping term, ensuring that the model does not drift too far from a well-behaved initialization.''}\end{quote}
The KL term is what saves RLHF from the reward-hacking pathology. It is the exact mechanism we analysed in our safety angle, applied to a frontier-scale model. Week 18 implements this end-to-end.
